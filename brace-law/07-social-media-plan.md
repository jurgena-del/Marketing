### TIKTOK — Scale What's Already Working

Brace Law already has 13.4K followers and 243K likes. This is the most undermonetized asset in the entire marketing mix.

**Current problem:** High engagement but no clear funnel from TikTok to consultation booking.

**Actions:**

1. Add a single strong CTA to every TikTok video bio and caption:
   - "Book a free consultation — link in bio"
   - Pin the link to the consultation page or a lead capture form

2. Post frequency: minimum 4x per week (currently inconsistent)

3. Content pillars for TikTok (rotate through these):
   - **"Did You Know?"** — quick immigration facts (30–45 seconds)
     - "Did you know a visa refusal doesn't mean the end? Here's what to do next."
     - "Did you know you can sponsor your parents to come to Canada? Here's how."
   - **"Case Type Explained"** — one immigration or family law topic per video
     - "What is an open work permit vs. a closed work permit?"
     - "What happens if your spousal sponsorship is refused?"
   - **"Myth vs. Fact"** — bust common immigration myths
     - "Myth: You don't need a lawyer for Express Entry. Fact: Here's why you might."
   - **"Story Time"** — anonymized client journey (no names, no case details)
     - "A client came to us after being refused twice. Here's what we did differently."
   - **"Team Behind the Law"** — show personality of Lusi Brace and the team
     - Day in the life, office culture, meet the lawyers
   - **"News Update"** — IRCC policy changes, processing time updates, immigration news

4. Use trending audio where appropriate — law content with trending sounds performs significantly better

5. Engage with comments within the first 60 minutes of every post — TikTok algorithm rewards early engagement

6. Duet and stitch relevant immigration news videos to add expert commentary

7. TikTok Live — go live once a week for 20–30 minutes:
   - "Ask an Immigration Lawyer" Q&A
   - Announce at least 48 hours in advance
   - Pin a comment with booking link during the live

8. TikTok Series feature — create a named series:
   - "Immigration in 60 Seconds"
   - "Family Law Explained"
   - "Hamilton Law Tips"

9. TikTok Lead Generation:
   - Set up TikTok Lead Gen ads targeting Ontario users aged 25–55 who follow immigration, newcomer, or legal content
   - Budget: CA$300–500/month test budget

10. Track: views per video, profile visits, link-in-bio clicks, consultation form submissions from TikTok traffic (use UTM parameters)

### INSTAGRAM — Tighten Strategy and Convert

5,543 followers with 1,172 posts means a lot of content that may not be working strategically. The "Brace Law Briefs" series is a strong asset.

**Actions:**

1. Audit all existing posts — identify top 10 by engagement, double down on those content types

2. Bio optimization — current bio must include:
   - What the firm does (immigration + family law)
   - Where (Hamilton, Vaughan, Oakville, GTAH)
   - One CTA with link (Linktree or direct booking page)
   - Example: "Immigration & Family Law Lawyers 🇨🇦 | Hamilton · Vaughan · Oakville | Free Consultation 👇"

3. Pinned posts (top 3 slots):
   - Pin 1: Firm intro / who we are video
   - Pin 2: Most-viewed Brace Law Briefs episode
   - Pin 3: Client testimonial or award announcement

4. Content mix (weekly):
   - 3x Reels (short video — highest reach on Instagram)
   - 2x Carousels (educational — "5 things to know about spousal sponsorship")
   - 1x Static post (award, team photo, office update)
   - 3–5x Stories daily (polls, Q&A stickers, behind the scenes, reminders)

5. Reels strategy — repurpose every TikTok video as an Instagram Reel (same content, both platforms)

6. Carousels that consistently perform for law firms:
   - "5 Reasons Your Immigration Application Gets Refused"
   - "What to Do If Your Canadian Visa Is Denied — Step by Step"
   - "Immigration vs. Immigration Consultant — What's the Difference?"
   - "3 Signs You Need a Family Lawyer, Not Mediation"
   - "Hamilton's Immigration Law Explained in 6 Slides"

7. Instagram Stories — daily minimum:
   - Monday: "Start the week" tip
   - Wednesday: Poll ("Have you ever dealt with an immigration refusal? Yes / No")
   - Friday: Team feature or office moment
   - Daily: Repost client Google reviews as Stories

8. Highlights to create/update:
   - "Immigration" — key services
   - "Family Law" — key services
   - "Our Team" — lawyer profiles
   - "Client Stories" — testimonials (with permission)
   - "Awards" — recognition and media features
   - "Free Consultation" — how to book

9. Instagram Broadcast Channel — create one for immigration news updates:
   - Subscribers get direct messages about IRCC changes, processing times, policy updates
   - Positions Brace Law as the go-to authority

10. Instagram Ads:
    - Boost top-performing Reels to Ontario audiences
    - Target: ages 25–54, Ontario, interests: immigration, newcomers to Canada, legal services
    - Budget: CA$200–400/month
    - Always drive to consultation booking page, not the homepage

11. Collaborate with:
    - Settlement agencies' Instagram accounts
    - Newcomer community pages
    - Ethnic food/culture pages in Hamilton, Vaughan, Brampton (cross-promo)
    - Ask Lusi Brace to appear on other lawyers' or community pages as a guest expert

### FACEBOOK — Activate the Community Channel

606 followers is very low for a multi-office law firm. Facebook is where immigrant communities (South Asian, Filipino, African, Arab) organize in Ontario. This is a missed channel.

**Actions:**

1. Complete the Facebook Page fully:
   - Professional cover photo (office or team)
   - Updated "About" section with all offices, phone, hours, services
   - Add all practice area tags
   - Enable reviews (if not already on)
   - Add "Book Now" or "Contact Us" button prominently

2. Post frequency: 4–5x per week minimum

3. Content — same as Instagram but Facebook-specific additions:
   - Share blog articles from the website
   - Share IRCC news and explain what it means for clients
   - Share client success stories (with consent)
   - Post event announcements (community seminars, free consultations)
   - Go live on Facebook for Q&A sessions

4. Join and participate in relevant Facebook Groups (do not spam — add value):
   - "Newcomers to Canada" groups
   - Hamilton, Vaughan, Brampton community groups
   - South Asian, Filipino, African community groups in Ontario
   - Real estate and settlement groups
   - Post helpful answers when immigration questions come up; do not directly advertise

5. Create a Facebook Group:
   - "Immigration Questions — Hamilton & GTA | Brace Law"
   - Invite existing clients and community
   - Answer questions regularly — builds trust and authority
   - Use as a lead generation tool (people asking questions = warm leads)

6. Facebook Ads:
   - Run lead generation ads targeting Ontario newcomer communities
   - Ad format: Lead Form ad (captures name + phone without leaving Facebook)
   - Target: ages 25–55, Ontario, interests: immigration Canada, newcomers, work permits, PR Canada
   - Budget: CA$300–500/month
   - Test multilingual ads: English, Punjabi, Arabic, Tagalog, Spanish

7. Retargeting:
   - Install Facebook Pixel on the website
   - Retarget website visitors who did not book a consultation
   - Show them a testimonial ad or "Free Consultation" reminder ad

### LINKEDIN — Build the B2B Referral Engine

301 followers is extremely low. LinkedIn is not for client acquisition — it's for building a referral network with other professionals who send clients to immigration and family lawyers.

**Actions:**

1. Optimize the Company Page:
   - Professional banner image
   - Complete "About" with full firm description, practice areas, locations
   - Add all team members and ensure their LinkedIn profiles link back to Brace Law

2. Lusi Brace's personal LinkedIn — this matters more than the company page:
   - Professional headshot
   - Headline: "Principal Lawyer — Immigration & Family Law | Brace Law | Hamilton, Vaughan, Oakville"
   - Feature section: add CTV and Toronto Star media mentions
   - Post 3x per week from her personal account

3. LinkedIn content that drives referrals:
   - "What real estate agents should know about clients on work permits" (targets realtors)
   - "When your HR client has an immigration issue — how to help them" (targets HR professionals)
   - "5 questions accountants hear from newcomer clients that need a lawyer" (targets accountants)
   - Case study posts (anonymized): "How we helped a family sponsorship that was refused twice"
   - Commentary on IRCC policy changes — positions Lusi as the expert

4. Connection strategy — connect with:
   - Real estate agents in Hamilton, Vaughan, Oakville, Brampton
   - Mortgage brokers who work with newcomers and PR holders
   - HR managers and recruiters at companies hiring internationally
   - Settlement workers and newcomer agencies
   - Accountants serving newcomer communities
   - Corporate lawyers who don't do immigration
   - Criminal lawyers who refer immigration cases

5. Send personal outreach messages to each connection after connecting:
   - "Hi [name], I noticed you work with a lot of newcomer clients in Hamilton. I'm an immigration lawyer at Brace Law — happy to be a resource if your clients ever have immigration questions. Feel free to pass along my details."

6. LinkedIn Newsletter — "Canadian Immigration Updates":
   - Monthly newsletter sent to all LinkedIn followers and subscribers
   - Cover IRCC news, processing times, policy changes
   - End with: "Questions? Book a free consultation with Brace Law."

7. Target: grow LinkedIn to 1,000+ followers within 6 months through consistent posting and outreach

### PODCAST — "Brace for Impact" — Revive and Systematize

The podcast is an underutilized asset. A law firm podcast builds authority, SEO value, and client trust faster than almost any other content type.

**Actions:**

1. Establish a consistent release schedule — every 2 weeks minimum

2. Episode format (30–45 minutes):
   - Topic intro (5 min)
   - Deep dive explanation (20 min)
   - Real case scenario (anonymized) (10 min)
   - What listeners should do if they face this situation (5 min)
   - CTA: book a free consultation

3. Episode topic ideas:
   - "What to do when your Canadian visa is refused"
   - "Understanding Express Entry in 2026 — what's changed"
   - "Spousal sponsorship step by step — what really happens"
   - "Divorce in Canada when one spouse is not a citizen"
   - "Work permit to PR — the roadmap explained"
   - "Immigration refusals — the most common reasons and how to avoid them"
   - "Bringing your parents to Canada — all the options explained"

4. Guest episodes:
   - Settlement workers and newcomer centre directors
   - Real estate agents who work with newcomers
   - Financial advisors serving immigrant communities
   - Other lawyers (criminal, corporate) who send immigration referrals

5. Distribute on: Spotify, Apple Podcasts, Google Podcasts, Amazon Music, YouTube (video version)

6. Repurpose each episode:
   - 3–5 short clips for TikTok and Instagram Reels
   - 1 blog post summarizing the episode
   - 3 LinkedIn posts from the episode's key points
   - 1 Instagram carousel from the episode's main takeaways
